## Why

Closes #1606, the style-and-builders half of #1547 (buttons, selection controls and progress take the
developer's own content and a style of their own), a family slice of the feature #1545 under the epic #898
(1.0: the web SDK stable). The content half is #1607, which waits for #1577. #1580's design settled the shape
every family follows: the instance's style is the family's slot record, laid over the theme's with its
`Merge`, and what a component repeats has a builder.

Measured on main by the guard #1580 put on the tree: none of the eleven components of this family takes a style
of its own (eleven `style` entries in `buttons-selection-and-progress.baseline.txt`), and a radio group draws
each option as its name and nothing else. A developer who wants one button in another outline, a denser
checkbox in one form or an option drawn with a price beside its name has no way to say it but to copy the
component or to change the theme for the whole subtree.

## What Changes

- **A style of their own.** `Button`, `FormSubmit`, `IconButton`, `Chip`, `Checkbox`, `RadioGroup`, `Switch`,
  `Slider`, `ProgressBar`, `PullToRefresh` and `Skeleton` each take `style:`, of their family's slot record
  (`ButtonTheme`, `IconButtonTheme`, `ChipTheme`, `SelectionControlsTheme`, `ProgressTheme`), as an optional
  constructor parameter and a `Style` property of the same type. Each resolves
  `XTheme.Merge(theme.X, Style)`: what the instance names wins over the theme's slot, and what it leaves null
  is the slot's, as `Markdown` already does with `MarkdownTheme`. `FormSubmit` hands its style to the `Button`
  it builds. `ProgressBar` is retained across its parent's builds, so it adopts the style with the rest of its
  configuration.
- **An option builder.** `RadioGroup` takes `optionBuilder: Func<int, VisualNode>?`. The builder draws what
  an option shows; the group keeps its chrome (the ring, the row a press lands on, the one Tab stop) and the
  option's name in `options` stays its accessible name. The content takes the option's colour through
  `BoxStyle.Foreground`. Null draws the name, as today.
- **The factories mirror it.** `UI.Button`, `UI.Chip`, `UI.Checkbox`, `UI.Switch`, `UI.Slider`,
  `UI.ProgressBar`, `UI.IconButton` and `UI.Skeleton` take `style` in the constructor's place, ahead of the
  initializer tail they already had. `RadioGroup` had no factory, so its builder could only be written with
  `new`: `UI.RadioGroup` mirrors its constructor, with `disabled` as its tail, as `UI.Checkbox` has.
- **The guard shrinks.** The eleven `style` entries leave the family's file, and the options entry says the
  builder draws what an option shows. The five content entries now cite #1607.

What a developer writes:

```csharp
Button("Delete", Variant.Outline, style: new ButtonTheme { OutlineWidth = 2, MinWidth = 120 })
RadioGroup(plans, selected, onChanged, optionBuilder: i => Row(gap: 8, children: [Text(plans[i]), Text(prices[i])]))
```

Parts reached: the components (`eQuantic.UI.Components`), their transpiled twins in the runtime (regenerated by
eqc, no hand-written runtime change), the web realizer and Photon unchanged. The public surface moves: the
eleven constructors and eight factories widen, and `Style` (and `RadioGroup.OptionBuilder`) appear. The
developer surface (csproj, appsettings, templates) does not move.

**Breaks, preview.** Each widened constructor and factory replaces the old signature (`*REMOVED*` lines in the
change's PublicAPI file). A call that named its arguments, or passed only the leading ones, compiles unchanged.
Four factories take `style` ahead of their initializer tail, so a call that passed that tail by position
moves: `Switch(on, onChanged, "Dark mode")` becomes `Switch(on, onChanged, label: "Dark mode")`, and the same
for `Slider`'s `min` and `ProgressBar`'s `label`, and `Checkbox`'s `disabled`.

Principles followed (`docs/PRINCIPLES.md`): 15, every component takes the developer's content, builders and
style; the preview rule, a signature widens and the old one goes. None is bent.
